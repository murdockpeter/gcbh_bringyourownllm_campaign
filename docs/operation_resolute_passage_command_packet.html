01 · Commander’s Intent

Reopen the corridor without widening the war.

Intent statement. The coalition will exploit the temporary control gained during Silver Current to escort the bulk sustainment package west through Hormuz. We will preserve the logistics backbone by detecting and defeating the Iranian surface-air attack at standoff range, disrupting the designated coastal kill chain under positive control, and retaining a recoverable combat force. Success is a functioning corridor and a sustained forward force in Bahrain—not destruction for its own sake.

Expanded Purpose

Prevent Bahrain’s forward naval and air-defense posture from falling below contingency readiness. Airlift cannot deliver the required bulk fuel, VLS reloads, repair modules, and medical stores in time.

Key Tasks

  • Maintain a recognized maritime and air picture from assembly through western exit.
  • Keep at least five of six auxiliaries mission capable.
  • Lead with MCM assets and keep high-value units behind layered air defense.
  • Destroy at least six of nine hostile surface combatants from outside FAC gun range.
  • Neutralize at least three designated coastal kill-chain nodes.
  • Preserve carrier and land-based recovery options; retain a credible post-mission screen.

End State

  • Five or more auxiliaries clear the decisive Strait sector westbound.
  • Iranian surface interception is combat ineffective.
  • At least three sensor/missile nodes are neutralized.
  • BLUE can recover aircraft and continue escort operations.
  • No attacks occur outside designated hostile forces and sites.

02 · Naval METT-TC

The mission viewed through the maritime fight.

M

Mission

Escort six auxiliaries west through the Hormuz corridor while defeating the forces that can close it. Protect five, destroy six hostile surface combatants, and neutralize three coastal nodes.

Decisive operation: protected convoy passage. Surface and shore strikes are shaping or supporting actions unless they directly threaten the convoy.

E

Enemy

24 assets: five Kaman FACs, two Moudge frigates, two Tsunami missile boats, three Bastions, three Pantsirs, one S-300, Coastwatch radar, Bandar Abbas strip, four MiG-29s, and two Su-24Ms.

Most likely COACoastwatch and fighters classify/fix the screen; Su-24s and coastal launchers force defensive expenditure; surface units close from the west to finish damaged auxiliaries.
Most dangerous COAA near-simultaneous Oniks/Noor/P-20/Kh-59 salvo saturates the AAW screen while MiGs pin CAP and FACs penetrate to gun range.
T

Terrain & Weather

The narrow Strait compresses formations and weapon axes. Peninsula and island geometry creates radar shadow, late visual contacts, and channelization. Open water east and west favors long-range sensing and missile employment.

Scenario conditions: 1400 local, sea state 3. Specific visibility, wind, and merchant density are not modeled; treat them as commander’s assumptions rather than known facts.

T

Troops & Support Available

Six auxiliaries; five major AAW combatants; Type 23, Cyclone, and cutter close screen; two MCMs; offshore carrier; Al Dhafra; eight fighters/strike aircraft; two P-8s; E-2D; KC-135R.

Constraint: player-controlled aircraft lose automatic attack tasking at game start. CAP and shore strike require deliberate player control and target assignment.

T

Time Available

The campaign premise gives less than twenty-four hours before Bahrain’s contingency stocks become operationally limiting. The tactical matrix is event-driven: phase on classification, salvo defeat, and geographic clearance rather than arbitrary clock time.

There is no formal in-game TimeGoal. The time pressure is operational context and a group pacing mechanism.

C

Civil Considerations

Hormuz is an international commercial chokepoint bordered by populated coasts. Maintain positive identification, avoid neutral shipping lanes where possible, and confine shore attack to the five designated nodes.

For Discord play, publish one command net, one air-control net, a shared target-status format, and a clear pause/clarification procedure before launch.

R

Risk to Mission / Risk to Force

Primary risk: convoy compression during a coordinated saturation attack. Control: do not outrun the MCM lead, maintain AAW depth, keep auxiliaries outside direct-fire range, and prioritize inbound weapons and shooters over opportunistic targets. Residual risk: one auxiliary may be lost without mission failure, but loss of two breaks Commander’s Intent.

03 · Maritime OCOKA

Read water, airspace, coastline, and sensors as one terrain system.

O

Observation & Fields of Fire

  • Sentinel and P-8 orbits provide the broad BLUE picture.
  • Coastwatch and S-300 anchor RED cueing.
  • The channel produces long missile fields but shortens reaction time around land masks.
  • Visual/FAC gun engagements indicate the screen has allowed excessive closure.
C

Cover & Concealment

  • Open sea gives little physical cover to the convoy.
  • Musandam and islands can mask radar and missile seekers.
  • RED surface units can emerge late from coastal clutter.
  • BLUE substitutes layered defense, emission control, spacing, and standoff for physical cover.
O

Obstacles

  • The Strait itself is the dominant channelizing obstacle.
  • Mine uncertainty justifies Devastator and Dextrous leading the passage.
  • Overlapping Bastion, S-300, and Pantsir zones constrain maneuver.
  • Neutral traffic and restrictive identification requirements limit free fire.
K

Key Terrain

  • Eastern assembly area: organize without coastal exposure.
  • Center lane: decisive geographic point and maximum compression.
  • Western exit: convoy clearance and transition point.
  • Coastwatch/Bastion clusters: functional key terrain for RED’s kill chain.
  • Carrier recovery box: preserves Tiger flight without committing the carrier.
A

Avenues of Approach

  • BLUE main: MCM-led westbound surface corridor.
  • BLUE air: CAP over corridor; strike axis from Al Dhafra; P-8 classification orbit east.
  • RED surface: eastbound blocking line from the western narrows.
  • RED air: MiGs fix CAP while Fencers approach the auxiliaries.
  • RED fires: north-to-south coastal missile axes into a compressed formation.

06 · Commander’s Critical Decisions

Four moments where the group should slow down and decide.

DP 1 · CommitIs the recognized picture good enough to move six auxiliaries into the channel, or should the convoy hold east?
DP 2 · Defend or strikeAre Lancer/Wildcat needed against the active coastal kill chain, or must they support the air/surface defense?
DP 3 · Continue or extractAfter the first massed salvo, can five auxiliaries still clear with a viable AAW screen?
DP 4 · TerminateOnce the corridor is open, does further pursuit add campaign value or merely create avoidable losses and escalation?
Discord facilitator note. Pause briefly at each decision point. Have the commander state the decision, supporting facts, accepted risk, and next phase trigger. That creates a clean shared record for the campaign AAR and gives every player a common mental model.
