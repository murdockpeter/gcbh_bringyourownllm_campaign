Executive summary

A layered check, not a single guess

The framework never executes an untrusted scenario to draw the map. It parses structured unit positions, domains, alliances, and waypoints from the Python source. Naval elements are recognized through their Ship1 tasking, with regional class lists retained as a compatibility fallback.

Every identified naval start and route is checked against global land polygons. When a scenario falls inside a supported regional theater, it also receives a second, game-oriented safe-water-mask check. A scenario outside that theater—such as Senkaku—is not incorrectly tested against Hormuz geometry.

Detection layers

What is checked

Real-world coastline

Global Natural Earth land and minor-island polygons detect starts on land, exact route/coast intersections, and near-coast clearance. A two-degree spatial grid and latitude-band index keep the global check responsive.

Regional GCBH safe-water mask

The hand-authored mask models water expected to be navigable in the game. It is activated only when the scenario center falls within that mask’s declared coverage bounds.

Candidate repair

Radial searches locate water with sufficient clearance. Route crossings may receive a detour only after both replacement legs are proven free of coastline intersections.

Optional Google corroboration

Elevation samples above +2 metres on a naval route are flagged as possible land. This is a secondary signal because coastal interpolation is not authoritative.

Geometry mechanics

How a route leg becomes evidence

A route is assembled as the unit’s start position followed by its ordered waypoints. For each adjacent pair, the framework checks the route segment against nearby coastline segments selected from the spatial index. This produces the first exact crossing point, if one exists.

route = [unit.position, ...unit.waypoints] for each leg (A → B): crossing = firstLandIntersection(A, B, globalLandIndex) clearance = min(distanceToLand(sample)) at 0.25 nm intervals

The requested clearance is user-selectable at 0.5, 1, 3, or 5 nautical miles. A negative signed distance means a point lies on land; zero or a small positive value means it is on or close to the coastline. Exact intersection and sampled clearance serve different purposes: the former detects crossing, while the latter identifies unsafe proximity even when the route stays technically offshore.
